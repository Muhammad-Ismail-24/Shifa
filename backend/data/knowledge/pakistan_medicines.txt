Knowledge Base: Common OTC Medicines Available in Pakistani Pharmacies
Compiled for RAG ingestion. Reference standards: DRAP National Essential Medicine List (NEML)
2025, WHO Model List of Essential Medicines, and publicly available product/package-insert
information for widely sold Pakistani OTC brands.

GENERAL SAFETY DISCLAIMER (applies to every entry below):
This information is for general educational reference only and is NOT a substitute for
consultation with a licensed pharmacist or physician. Dosages listed are standard adult
guidance for short-term, occasional OTC use as commonly printed on Pakistani product
packaging; they are NOT appropriate for every individual. Always check the product leaflet,
consult a pharmacist/doctor before use — especially for children, pregnant or breastfeeding
women, the elderly, and people with kidney, liver, heart, or bleeding conditions — and never
exceed the maximum stated daily dose. If symptoms persist beyond 3 days (fever) or worsen at
any point, seek medical attention. Many "common" Pakistani brand names listed are Schedule
OTC/GSL (general sales list) products per DRAP classification; some formulations of the same
generic (e.g., higher-strength metronidazole, certain antibiotics) legally require a
prescription in Pakistan even though enforcement varies in practice — a RAG system should not
encourage self-medication with antibiotics without medical supervision.
Sources: [DRAP] National Essential Medicine List 2025 — https://www.dra.gov.pk; WHO Model List
of Essential Medicines (used as the template DRAP's own EML is explicitly based on, per Punjab
Department of Health EML documentation).

================================================================================
1. PARACETAMOL (ACETAMINOPHEN) — Pain relief / fever reducer
================================================================================
Local brand names: Panadol, Panadol Extra, Calpol (pediatric syrup), Tylenol, Paracip
Treats: Mild-to-moderate pain, headache, fever, body aches, menstrual cramps
Standard adult dosage: 500–1000 mg every 4–6 hours as needed; maximum 4000 mg (4 g) in 24
hours (lower max advised for those with liver disease or regular alcohol use)
Pediatric: Weight-based dosing (typically ~10–15 mg/kg per dose) — use pediatric
syrup/suspension per package instructions; do not use adult tablets for young children.
Safety notes: Overdose can cause serious liver damage; avoid combining multiple products that
each contain paracetamol (common cause of accidental overdose).
Reference: WHO Model List of Essential Medicines includes paracetamol as a core analgesic/
antipyretic; DRAP NEML lists paracetamol among essential medicines.

================================================================================
2. IBUPROFEN — NSAID pain reliever / anti-inflammatory
================================================================================
Local brand names: Brufen, Advil, Nurofen
Treats: Pain, inflammation, fever, muscle/joint pain, menstrual cramps
Standard adult dosage: 200–400 mg every 6–8 hours with food; maximum 1200 mg/day for OTC use
(higher doses only under medical supervision)
Safety notes: Avoid in peptic ulcer disease, kidney impairment, and in the third trimester of
pregnancy; take with food to reduce stomach irritation; avoid combining with aspirin regularly.
Reference: WHO Model List of Essential Medicines lists ibuprofen as an essential NSAID.

================================================================================
3. ASPIRIN — Pain reliever / antiplatelet
================================================================================
Local brand names: Disprin, Ecosprin (low-dose cardioprotective form)
Treats: Mild pain, fever (higher dose); low-dose used long-term for cardiovascular protection
under medical supervision
Standard adult dosage: 300–600 mg every 4–6 hours for pain/fever (max ~4g/day); low-dose 75–
150 mg once daily only when prescribed for cardiovascular prevention
Safety notes: NOT recommended for children/teenagers with viral illness (risk of Reye's
syndrome); avoid in active bleeding disorders or peptic ulcers; do not use for fever in
suspected dengue (increases bleeding risk) — use paracetamol instead.
Reference: WHO Model List of Essential Medicines includes acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin).

================================================================================
4. ORAL REHYDRATION SALTS (ORS)
================================================================================
Local brand names: WHO-ORS sachets, ORSaline, Rehydran
Treats: Dehydration from diarrhea/vomiting
Standard adult/child dosage: One sachet dissolved in the specified volume of clean water
(typically 200 ml or 1 liter per packaging instructions) — sip frequently; replace ongoing
fluid losses.
Safety notes: Use exact water volume specified on the packet; do not add sugar/salt beyond
formulation; seek medical care immediately if severe dehydration signs are present (see
emergency_warning_signs.txt).
Reference: WHO/UNICEF standard ORS formulation is on the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines
and is DRAP NEML-listed as a first-line diarrhea management product.

================================================================================
5. LOPERAMIDE — Anti-diarrheal
================================================================================
Local brand names: Imodium, Lopamide
Treats: Acute non-bloody diarrhea (symptomatic relief)
Standard adult dosage: 4 mg initially, then 2 mg after each loose stool; maximum 16 mg/day
Safety notes: Do NOT use if fever or blood in stool is present (may worsen bacterial dysentery);
not recommended for children under 12 without medical advice; use alongside ORS, not instead
of it.
Reference: WHO Model List of Essential Medicines lists loperamide for symptomatic diarrhea
management.

================================================================================
6. METRONIDAZOLE — Antiprotozoal / antibacterial
================================================================================
Local brand names: Flagyl, Metrogyl
Treats: Amoebic dysentery, giardiasis, certain bacterial (anaerobic) infections
Standard adult dosage: Typically 400 mg two to three times daily for 5–7 days depending on
indication — dosage and duration vary by infection type
Safety notes: This is a prescription-intent antimicrobial in most regulatory frameworks; should
be taken only on medical/pharmacist advice with correct diagnosis and full course completed to
avoid resistance. Avoid alcohol during and for 48 hours after treatment (disulfiram-like
reaction). A responsible RAG system should route the user toward consulting a healthcare
provider rather than self-prescribing antimicrobials.
Reference: WHO Model List of Essential Medicines includes metronidazole as an essential
antiprotozoal/antibacterial agent; DRAP regulates antimicrobial sale under its Drugs Act 1976
framework.

================================================================================
7. ANTACIDS / H2-BLOCKERS — Acid reflux / indigestion relief
================================================================================
Local brand names: Gaviscon, ENO, Risek (omeprazole, technically a PPI — see note), Zantac
(ranitidine, withdrawn/restricted in many markets due to NDMA contamination concerns —
check current DRAP status before recommending)
Treats: Heartburn, acid reflux, indigestion
Standard adult dosage: Antacid liquids/tablets as needed after meals per label; omeprazole 20
mg once daily (short-term OTC use, typically up to 14 days) for reflux symptoms
Safety notes: Persistent symptoms beyond 2 weeks, or symptoms with weight loss/difficulty
swallowing/vomiting blood, require medical evaluation, not continued self-treatment.
Reference: WHO Model List of Essential Medicines includes omeprazole as an essential
proton-pump inhibitor.

================================================================================
8. CETIRIZINE / LORATADINE — Antihistamines
================================================================================
Local brand names: Zyrtec, Alerid, Claritin
Treats: Allergic rhinitis, hives, itching
Standard adult dosage: Cetirizine 10 mg once daily; Loratadine 10 mg once daily
Safety notes: Cetirizine may cause drowsiness in some individuals (loratadine less so);
avoid driving/operating machinery until effect is known; use pediatric formulations for
children.
Reference: WHO Model List of Essential Medicines includes chlorphenamine and cetirizine among
recommended antihistamines.

================================================================================
9. AMOXICILLIN / CO-AMOXICLAV — Antibiotics
================================================================================
Local brand names: Augmentin (co-amoxiclav), Amoxil
Treats: Bacterial infections (respiratory, ear, throat, urinary, skin)
Standard adult dosage: Amoxicillin 500 mg every 8 hours; Augmentin dosing varies by strength
(e.g., 625 mg every 8 hours) — course length set by prescriber, typically 5–10 days
Safety notes: These are prescription antibiotics; must be taken only when prescribed for a
confirmed or strongly suspected bacterial infection, with the full course completed even if
symptoms improve early, to reduce antimicrobial resistance — a growing public health concern
in Pakistan. A RAG system should direct users to a physician rather than provide antibiotic
self-dosing guidance.
Reference: WHO Model List of Essential Medicines; DRAP antimicrobial stewardship guidelines
flag rising resistance as a national priority.

================================================================================
10. VITAMIN/MINERAL SUPPLEMENTS
================================================================================
Local brand names: Surbex-Z, Neurobion, Ferrous sulfate/folic acid combinations (e.g.,
"Folivit"), Calcium-D3 supplements (e.g., "Caltrate")
Treats: Vitamin B-complex deficiency, iron-deficiency anemia (ferrous sulfate/folic acid),
calcium/vitamin D deficiency
Standard adult dosage: Varies widely by product and indication — follow package instructions;
iron/folic acid supplementation in pregnancy typically follows national antenatal care
protocol dosing (as advised by an obstetric care provider).
Safety notes: Iron supplements can cause constipation/GI upset and are dangerous in overdose,
especially for children (keep out of reach); do not exceed labeled dose without medical advice.
Reference: DRAP NEML and WHO antenatal care guidelines recommend iron-folic acid
supplementation for pregnant women given the high prevalence of maternal anemia in Pakistan.

================================================================================
11. ORAL ANTISEPTIC / COUGH & COLD PREPARATIONS
================================================================================
Local brand names: Betadine (povidone-iodine, topical antiseptic), Strepsils (throat lozenges),
Corex/Tixylix-type cough syrups (composition varies — check for codeine/opioid content, which
is prescription-controlled in Pakistan)
Treats: Minor wound antisepsis, sore throat, cough/cold symptomatic relief
Standard adult dosage: Topical antiseptic — apply as needed to clean, intact skin around a
wound; lozenges as directed on package (typically every 2–3 hours, max per label)
Safety notes: Codeine-containing cough syrups are subject to DRAP controlled-substance
restrictions due to misuse potential — a RAG system should not provide dosing guidance for
opioid-containing cough preparations and should direct such queries to a pharmacist/physician.
Reference: DRAP controlled drugs scheduling framework under the Drugs Act, 1976 and Control of
Narcotic Substances Act.

GENERAL DRAP CONTEXT NOTE
================================================================================
DRAP (Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan), established in 2012 under the DRAP Act, regulates
registration, quality, safety, and efficacy of all therapeutic goods sold in Pakistan, including
the Health & OTC product category. Its National Essential Medicine List (most recently updated
2025) defines which medicines are considered essential for primary healthcare needs, ensuring
availability, appropriate dosage forms, and assured quality nationwide.
Reference: [DRAP] "National Essential Medicine Lists," https://www.dra.gov.pk/publications/
national-essential-medicine-lists/ ; [DRAP] "Model List of National Essential Medicines
(2025) – Notification Dated 20-10-2025."
