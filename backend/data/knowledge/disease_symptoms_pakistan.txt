DISEASE_SYMPTOMS_PAKISTAN.TXT
Knowledge Base: Top 25 Diseases in Pakistan — Symptoms, Severity, Emergency Signs, Demographics
Compiled for RAG ingestion. Sources cited per entry. Last compiled: August 2026.
DISCLAIMER: This file is for informational/triage-support purposes only. It does not replace
professional medical diagnosis. Any user-facing system built on this data must direct users
with severe/emergency symptoms to seek immediate in-person care (Rescue 1122 in Punjab/KP,
or nearest hospital emergency department).

1. ISCHEMIC HEART DISEASE (IHD) / HEART ATTACK (Dil Ka Daura)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Chest pain or pressure, pain radiating to left arm/jaw/back, shortness of
breath, sweating, nausea, fatigue.
Urdu symptoms: سینے میں درد یا دباؤ (seenay mein dard ya dabao), بائیں بازو میں درد (baayen
baazu mein dard), سانس پھولنا (saans phoolna), پسینہ آنا (paseena aana), متلی (matli)
Severity: Severe to Emergency
Emergency warning signs: Crushing central chest pain >15 minutes, pain with sweating and
breathlessness, fainting, irregular/racing heartbeat — call for emergency transport immediately.
Affected demographics: Adults 40+, higher risk in males, urban populations, diabetics,
hypertensive and smoking populations.
Source: [GBD1] — ischemic heart disease listed among the top five leading causes of premature
mortality in Pakistan in 2019.

================================================================================
2. STROKE (Falij / Fatoor)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Sudden weakness/numbness of face, arm, or leg (usually one side), slurred
speech, confusion, sudden severe headache, vision loss, loss of balance.
Urdu symptoms: اچانک ایک طرف کمزوری (achanak aik taraf kamzori), بولنے میں دقت (bolne mein
diqqat), چہرے کا ٹیڑھا ہونا (chehray ka terha hona), شدید سر درد (shadeed sar dard)
Severity: Emergency
Emergency warning signs: FAST signs — Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to
call emergency services; sudden loss of consciousness.
Affected demographics: Adults over 50, hypertensive patients, diabetics; slightly more common
in men but significant burden in women too.
Source: [GBD1] — stroke among top five causes of premature mortality nationally.

================================================================================
3. NEONATAL DISORDERS / BIRTH ASPHYXIA & PREMATURITY (Nomulodi Bachay ki Bimari)
================================================================================
English symptoms (newborn): Difficulty breathing, weak/absent cry, poor feeding, low body
temperature, bluish skin (cyanosis), lethargy, convulsions.
Urdu symptoms: سانس لینے میں دقت (saans lenay mein diqqat), دودھ نہ پینا (doodh na peena),
جسم کا نیلا ہونا (jisam ka neela hona), سستی (susti)
Severity: Emergency (in newborns, always treat as high-risk)
Emergency warning signs: Any newborn not breathing well, not feeding, convulsing, or
unresponsive needs immediate hospital care.
Affected demographics: Neonates (0–28 days), especially in rural areas with limited access to
skilled birth attendants.
Source: [GBD1] — neonatal disorders were the number one cause of premature mortality in
Pakistan in 2019.

================================================================================
4. DIARRHEAL DISEASE / ACUTE GASTROENTERITIS (Dast / Ishaal)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Frequent loose/watery stools, abdominal cramps, vomiting, fever, signs of
dehydration.
Urdu symptoms: پتلے دست (patlay dast), پیٹ میں مروڑ (pait mein maror), قے (qai), بخار (bukhar)
Severity: Mild to Emergency (depends on dehydration level)
Emergency warning signs: Sunken eyes, no urination for 6+ hours, extreme thirst or inability to
drink, lethargy/unconsciousness, blood in stool — signs of severe dehydration requiring
immediate IV fluids.
Affected demographics: Children under 5 disproportionately affected; also common in adults
during monsoon/flood season due to contaminated water.
Source: [GBD1] — diarrheal diseases among top five causes of premature mortality in Pakistan.

================================================================================
5. LOWER RESPIRATORY INFECTIONS / PNEUMONIA (Nimonia)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Cough, fever, fast or difficult breathing, chest indrawing, chills.
Urdu symptoms: کھانسی (khansi), بخار (bukhar), سانس تیز چلنا (saans tez chalna), سینہ دھنسنا
(seena dhansna)
Severity: Moderate to Emergency
Emergency warning signs (IMCI danger signs): Chest indrawing, grunting, inability to
drink/breastfeed, bluish lips, oxygen saturation dropping.
Affected demographics: Young children (leading killer of under-5s globally with pneumonia),
elderly, and malnourished individuals.
Source: [GBD1]; [IMCI] danger-sign criteria for severe pneumonia.

================================================================================
6. TUBERCULOSIS (TB) (Tapedik / TB)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Cough lasting more than 2 weeks, blood in sputum, night sweats, weight loss,
fever, fatigue.
Urdu symptoms: دو ہفتوں سے زیادہ کھانسی (do haftoun se zyada khansi), رات کو پسینہ آنا (raat
ko paseena aana), وزن کم ہونا (wazan kam hona), بلغم میں خون (balgham mein khoon)
Severity: Moderate to Severe
Emergency warning signs: Coughing up large amounts of blood, severe difficulty breathing,
extreme weakness — refer urgently.
Affected demographics: All ages; Pakistan is among the top TB-burden countries. Higher risk in
crowded housing, malnutrition, and immunocompromised individuals.
Source: [WHO1] — Pakistan accounted for approximately 6.3% of global TB cases in 2024, with an
estimated 670,000 new cases and roughly 51,000 TB deaths annually.

================================================================================
7. HEPATITIS B (Kaala Yarqaan / Hepatitis B)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Fatigue, jaundice (yellow skin/eyes), dark urine, abdominal pain, nausea,
loss of appetite; often asymptomatic for years.
Urdu symptoms: جسم کا زرد ہونا (jism ka zard hona), گہرا پیشاب (gehra peshab), تھکاوٹ
(thakawat), بھوک نہ لگنا (bhook na lagna)
Severity: Mild (often silent) to Severe (in chronic liver disease/cirrhosis)
Emergency warning signs: Severe abdominal swelling, confusion, vomiting blood, deep jaundice —
signs of liver failure requiring urgent hospital care.
Affected demographics: General population; higher prevalence linked to unsafe injections,
transfusions, and unscreened blood products.
Source: [NIH2] — general-population hepatitis B prevalence in Pakistan estimated at 1.6%, with
about 12,000 deaths/year attributed to HBV and its complications.

================================================================================
8. HEPATITIS C (Hepatitis C)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Similar to Hepatitis B — fatigue, jaundice, joint pain; frequently
asymptomatic until advanced liver damage.
Urdu symptoms: جسم کا زرد ہونا (jism ka zard hona), جوڑوں کا درد (joroon ka dard), کمزوری
(kamzori)
Severity: Mild (silent) to Severe (cirrhosis/liver cancer risk)
Emergency warning signs: Vomiting blood, black tarry stools, severe abdominal distension,
confusion (hepatic encephalopathy).
Affected demographics: Adults; Pakistan has one of the highest HCV burdens in the world, linked
to reuse of syringes and unsafe medical practices.
Source: [NIH2] — hepatitis C prevalence in the general population estimated at 7.5%, with
around 19,000 deaths/year and roughly 545,000 new infections annually.

================================================================================
9. DENGUE FEVER (Dengue Bukhar)
================================================================================
English symptoms: High fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, joint/muscle pain, rash,
mild bleeding (nose/gums).
Urdu symptoms: تیز بخار (tez bukhar), آنکھوں کے پیچھے درد (aankhon ke peechay dard), جوڑوں کا
درد (joroon ka dard), جسم پر دانے (jism par daanay)
Severity: Moderate to Emergency (severe dengue/dengue hemorrhagic fever)
Emergency warning signs: Severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding gums/nose, blood
in vomit or stool, difficulty breathing, extreme fatigue/restlessness — signs of severe dengue
requiring immediate hospitalization.
Affected demographics: All ages, seasonal spikes (monsoon, especially in Punjab and Sindh urban
centers).
Source: [WHO2]; national dengue surveillance reports referenced by Pakistan's Ministry of
National Health Services.

================================================================================
10. MALARIA (Malaria)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Cyclical high fever with chills and rigors, sweating, headache, muscle aches,
nausea.
Urdu symptoms: بخار کے ساتھ کپکپی (bukhar ke sath kapkapi), سر درد (sar dard), جسم درد (jism
dard)
Severity: Moderate to Emergency (cerebral/severe malaria)
Emergency warning signs: Seizures, confusion, unconsciousness, extreme pallor/anemia,
difficulty breathing — signs of severe/cerebral malaria.
Affected demographics: Rural and flood-affected populations, especially in Sindh and Balochistan.
Source: [GBD1] general infectious-disease burden context; WHO World Malaria Report country data.

================================================================================
11. TYPHOID FEVER (Mote Jhamke / Typhoid)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Sustained high fever, weakness, stomach pain, headache, loss of appetite,
constipation or diarrhea, rose-colored rash.
Urdu symptoms: مسلسل بخار (musalsal bukhar), پیٹ میں درد (pait mein dard), کمزوری (kamzori),
بھوک کی کمی (bhook ki kami)
Severity: Moderate to Severe
Emergency warning signs: Severe abdominal pain with rigidity (possible intestinal perforation),
confusion, blood in stool.
Affected demographics: Children and young adults, linked to contaminated food/water; extensively
drug-resistant (XDR) typhoid strains reported in Sindh.
Source: [GBD1]; WHO typhoid fever fact sheet.

================================================================================
12. COVID-19 (Corona Virus)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Fever, dry cough, fatigue, loss of taste/smell, sore throat, shortness of
breath.
Urdu symptoms: بخار (bukhar), خشک کھانسی (khushk khansi), ذائقہ اور سونگھنے کی حس ختم ہونا
(zaiqa aur songhne ki hiss khatam hona), سانس لینے میں دشواری (saans lenay mein dushwari)
Severity: Mild to Emergency
Emergency warning signs: Persistent chest pain/pressure, bluish lips/face, confusion, oxygen
saturation below 92%, inability to stay awake.
Affected demographics: All ages; elderly and those with comorbidities (diabetes, heart/lung
disease) at highest risk of severe disease.
Source: [WHO2]; WHO COVID-19 clinical management guidance.

================================================================================
13. TYPE 2 DIABETES MELLITUS (Sugar / Ziyabetus)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Excessive thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss, fatigue,
blurred vision, slow-healing wounds.
Urdu symptoms: زیادہ پیاس لگنا (zyada pyaas lagna), بار بار پیشاب آنا (baar baar peshab aana),
دھندلا نظر آنا (dhundla nazar aana), زخم کا دیر سے بھرنا (zakhm ka dair se bharna)
Severity: Mild (controlled) to Emergency (diabetic ketoacidosis, hypoglycemia)
Emergency warning signs: Confusion, fruity-smelling breath, rapid breathing, unconsciousness
(DKA); or sweating, shaking, confusion from severe low blood sugar (hypoglycemia).
Affected demographics: Adults, rising prevalence in urban populations; Pakistan has one of the
highest diabetes prevalence rates in the world per International Diabetes Federation data.
Source: [GBD1]/[NCD] — NCDs including diabetes are rising sharply and forecast to be among the
top three causes of death in Pakistan in coming decades.

================================================================================
14. HYPERTENSION (High Blood Pressure / Balaa Fasha Khoon)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Often asymptomatic; may include headache, dizziness, nosebleeds when severe.
Urdu symptoms: عام طور پر کوئی علامت نہیں، شدید صورت میں سر درد (sar dard), چکر آنا (chakkar
aana), ناک سے خون (naak se khoon)
Severity: Mild (silent) to Emergency (hypertensive crisis)
Emergency warning signs: Blood pressure reading with severe headache, chest pain, vision
changes, confusion, or shortness of breath — hypertensive emergency.
Affected demographics: Adults, more common with age, obesity, high-salt diets; a leading NCD
risk factor nationally.
Source: [GBD1] — high systolic blood pressure listed among the top all-age risk factors for
death and disability in Pakistan.

================================================================================
15. CHRONIC OBSTRUCTIVE PULMONARY DISEASE (COPD) (Saans ki Purani Bimari)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Chronic cough, sputum production, breathlessness on exertion, wheezing.
Urdu symptoms: پرانی کھانسی (purani khansi), سانس پھولنا (saans phoolna), سینے میں گھرگھراہٹ
(seenay mein ghurghurahat)
Severity: Moderate to Emergency
Emergency warning signs: Severe breathlessness at rest, bluish lips/fingertips, confusion,
inability to speak full sentences.
Affected demographics: Smokers, biomass-fuel-exposed rural women, adults 40+.
Source: [GBD1] air pollution and tobacco listed among leading national risk factors.

================================================================================
16. CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE (CKD) (Guron ki Bimari)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Fatigue, swelling in legs/feet, reduced urine output, itching, nausea, poor
appetite.
Urdu symptoms: تھکاوٹ (thakawat), ٹانگوں میں سوجن (tangoon mein sujan), پیشاب کم آنا (peshab
kam aana), خارش (kharish)
Severity: Moderate to Emergency (end-stage renal failure)
Emergency warning signs: Little or no urination, severe shortness of breath, chest pain,
confusion, seizures — signs of uremia/fluid overload requiring dialysis.
Affected demographics: Diabetics, hypertensive patients, adults 40+.
Source: [GBD1]/NCD burden literature — CKD strongly linked to rising diabetes/hypertension
prevalence.

================================================================================
17. ASTHMA (Damma)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, cough (often worse at night).
Urdu symptoms: گھرگھراہٹ (ghurghurahat), سانس پھولنا (saans phoolna), سینے میں جکڑن (seenay
mein jakran)
Severity: Mild to Emergency (status asthmaticus)
Emergency warning signs: Severe difficulty speaking/breathing, bluish lips, drowsiness, no
improvement with usual inhaler.
Affected demographics: Children and adults; aggravated by urban air pollution and smoke exposure.
Source: [GBD1] air pollution risk-factor data.

================================================================================
18. MEASLES (Khasra)
================================================================================
English symptoms: High fever, cough, runny nose, red watery eyes, red blotchy rash starting on
face.
Urdu symptoms: تیز بخار (tez bukhar), کھانسی (khansi), آنکھوں کی سرخی (aankhon ki surkhi),
جسم پر دانے (jism par daanay)
Severity: Moderate to Emergency (complications: pneumonia, encephalitis)
Emergency warning signs: Difficulty breathing, persistent high fever, convulsions, extreme
lethargy.
Affected demographics: Unvaccinated children; periodic outbreaks linked to vaccination gaps.
Source: [WHO2]; national EPI (Expanded Programme on Immunization) surveillance data.

================================================================================
19. CHOLERA (Hazza)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Sudden onset of profuse watery ("rice-water") diarrhea, vomiting, rapid
dehydration, leg cramps.
Urdu symptoms: اچانک شدید پتلے دست (achanak shadeed patlay dast), قے (qai), ٹانگوں میں مروڑ
(tangoon mein maror)
Severity: Severe to Emergency
Emergency warning signs: Signs of severe dehydration — sunken eyes, no tears, extreme
lethargy, weak/absent pulse — can be fatal within hours without rehydration.
Affected demographics: Populations with poor water/sanitation access, flood-affected areas.
Source: [GBD1] diarrheal disease burden; WHO cholera fact sheet.

================================================================================
20. MALNUTRITION / STUNTING (Protein-Energy Malnutrition) (Kamzori-e-Ghiza)
================================================================================
English symptoms (children): Poor weight gain, visible wasting, swelling of feet (edema), thin
hair, low energy.
Urdu symptoms: وزن کا نہ بڑھنا (wazan ka na barhna), جسم کا لاغر ہونا (jism ka laghar hona),
پاؤں میں سوجن (paon mein sujan)
Severity: Moderate to Emergency (severe acute malnutrition)
Emergency warning signs: Severe wasting, bilateral pitting edema, apathy/lethargy, poor
appetite/refusal to eat — requires urgent therapeutic feeding.
Affected demographics: Children under 5, especially in Sindh and Balochistan; pregnant/lactating
women.
Source: [GBD1] — child and maternal malnutrition cited as the leading national risk factor for
death and disability-adjusted life-years.

================================================================================
21. BREAST CANCER (Cancer)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Breast lump, change in breast shape/size, skin dimpling, nipple discharge or
inversion, persistent breast pain.
Urdu symptoms: چھاتی میں گلٹی (chaati mein gulti), چھاتی کی شکل میں تبدیلی (chaati ki shakal
mein tabdeeli), نپل سے رطوبت (nipple se rutoobat)
Severity: Moderate to Severe (depends on stage at diagnosis)
Emergency warning signs: Rapidly enlarging mass, ulceration/bleeding from breast, severe bone
pain (possible metastasis) — urgent oncology referral.
Affected demographics: Women, typically 40+, though Pakistan has among the highest breast
cancer incidence rates in Asia with cases also seen in younger women.
Source: [GBD1]/[NCD] cancers listed among rising NCD causes of death nationally.

================================================================================
22. ROAD TRAFFIC INJURIES (Traffic Hadsat)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Trauma-related pain, visible wounds/fractures, bleeding, loss of
consciousness, difficulty moving limbs.
Urdu symptoms: شدید چوٹ (shadeed choat), خون بہنا (khoon behna), بے ہوشی (be-hoshi), ہڈی ٹوٹنا
(haddi tootna)
Severity: Moderate to Emergency
Emergency warning signs: Uncontrolled bleeding, loss of consciousness, difficulty breathing,
suspected spinal injury (do not move patient), severe deformity of limbs.
Affected demographics: Young adult males disproportionately affected (motorcyclists,
pedestrians); a top cause of injury-related mortality nationally.
Source: [GBD1] triple-burden-of-disease analysis includes injuries as one of Pakistan's three
major disease burden categories.

================================================================================
23. DEPRESSION & ANXIETY DISORDERS (Zehni Dabao / Depression)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Persistent sadness, loss of interest, fatigue, sleep disturbance, appetite
changes, feelings of worthlessness, difficulty concentrating.
Urdu symptoms: اداسی (udaasi), دل نہ لگنا (dil na lagna), نیند نہ آنا (neend na aana), بے چینی
(bechaini)
Severity: Mild to Emergency (with suicidal ideation)
Emergency warning signs: Expressed thoughts of self-harm or suicide, hopelessness with a plan
or intent — requires immediate mental-health crisis support/referral.
Affected demographics: All ages, notably high burden among women (postpartum depression) and
in populations facing socioeconomic stress or displacement.
Source: [GBD1] burden-of-disease analysis; WHO mental health atlas Pakistan country profile.

================================================================================
24. IRON-DEFICIENCY ANEMIA (Khoon ki Kami)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Fatigue, pale skin, dizziness, shortness of breath on exertion, brittle nails,
cold hands/feet.
Urdu symptoms: تھکاوٹ (thakawat), جسم کا پیلا پڑنا (jism ka peela parna), چکر آنا (chakkar
aana), سانس پھولنا (saans phoolna)
Severity: Mild to Severe
Emergency warning signs: Severe pallor with breathlessness at rest, rapid heartbeat, fainting —
may require urgent transfusion, especially in pregnancy.
Affected demographics: Women of reproductive age and pregnant women disproportionately
affected; also common in children.
Source: [GBD1] — dietary risk factors and maternal malnutrition listed among leading national
risk factors for disease burden.

================================================================================
25. POLIOMYELITIS (Polio)
================================================================================
English symptoms: Fever, fatigue, headache, vomiting, stiffness in neck/back, followed in some
cases by acute flaccid paralysis of limbs.
Urdu symptoms: بخار (bukhar), سر درد (sar dard), گردن کی اکڑن (gardan ki akarn), اچانک ہاتھ
پاؤں کا فالج (achanak hath paon ka falij)
Severity: Moderate to Emergency (paralytic polio)
Emergency warning signs: Sudden onset of limb weakness/paralysis, difficulty breathing or
swallowing (bulbar involvement) — requires immediate hospital referral and case reporting to
health authorities.
Affected demographics: Unvaccinated children under 5; Pakistan remains one of the last two
countries where wild poliovirus transmission is endemic.
Source: [WHO2]; WHO Global Polio Eradication Initiative Pakistan country data.
